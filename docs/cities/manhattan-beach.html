Interior Design for Manhattan Beach Homes

Manhattan Beach is one of Los Angeles's most desirable coastal addresses — and one of its most demanding design environments. The combination of strong natural light, salt air, and the physical proximity to the ocean means that material choices matter more here than almost anywhere else in the city. Finishes that look beautiful in a showroom can fail within two years in a beach home; furniture selections that work inland don't always translate to a house where sandy feet are the norm.

JAC Interiors has designed coastal homes throughout the South Bay, and we bring firsthand knowledge of what holds up, what photographs well, and what actually makes beach life comfortable and beautiful. We don't apply a generic coastal aesthetic — we design around how your specific household lives.

Designing for the Ocean Environment

The ocean environment is both the appeal and the constraint. We select upholstery fabrics rated for high UV exposure and easy cleaning. Cabinetry hardware gets specified in finishes that resist salt-air corrosion. Window treatments balance the desire for ocean views with the practical reality of fading — high-performance sheers and woven woods are workhorses in beach homes for a reason.

Indoor-outdoor flow is non-negotiable in Manhattan Beach. We design living spaces that open naturally onto decks, patios, and rooftop areas — using consistent material language across the threshold so the transition feels seamless. When your back door opens to the Strand, the interior should feel like a natural extension of the beach, not a retreat from it.

04

The Three Sections — and What Each Requires

The Sand Section

Walk streets, Strand-adjacent lots, and the highest density of true beachfront homes in the South Bay. Sand Section homes get the most sun, the most wind, and the most foot traffic — we prioritize durability, easy maintenance, and interiors that can take the wear without looking worn.

The Tree Section

Shaded streets, larger lots, and more traditional family-oriented architecture. Tree Section homes often have more interior square footage and benefit from a slightly warmer palette — still relaxed, but with more depth and layering than a beachfront property requires.

The Hill Section

Elevated lots with panoramic views of the bay and ocean. Hill Section homes are designed to maximize those views — large windows, outdoor terraces, and a palette that complements rather than competes with the water beyond.
